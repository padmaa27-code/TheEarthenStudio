What happens to your pot

Six steps, three weeks, two firings.

The order matters and none of it can be rushed. This is why your pots go home weeks after you do.

  1. 01

    Wedge

    Air pockets are knocked out of the clay. A trapped bubble becomes an explosion at 1000°C.

  2. 02

    Centre

    The hardest twenty minutes of the day. Everything after this is easy by comparison.

  3. 03

    Pull

    Walls rise between two fingers. Too fast and it collapses, and that is fine, you start again.

  4. 04

    Trim

    Four days later, leather-hard, we turn a foot into the base and stamp it.

  5. 05

    Bisque fire

    Cone 06, roughly 1000°C, twelve hours up and eighteen hours cooling.

  6. 06

    Glaze fire

    Cone 6, 1222°C. Glass forms on the surface. This is where the colour arrives.

Who runs it

Four people, one of whom is always covered in slip.

Two of us trained in Puducherry and one learned from her grandmother in Khurja, which is where most of what we know about reclaiming clay comes from. The fourth handles the kilns and the accounts, in that order of preference.

We teach the way we were taught: hands on your hands for the first pot, then progressively less until you are annoyed that nobody is helping. That is the point at which you have learned to throw.

Before you come

The things everyone asks.

What should I wear to a pottery session?

Something you would not mind ruining. Clay washes out of cotton and does not wash out of denim. Short sleeves, or sleeves you can push past the elbow. Take rings off, because clay gets under stones and stays there.

Why do the pots take three weeks?

Your pot has to dry, be trimmed, dry again to bone-dry, go through a bisque firing, be glazed, and go through a second firing at 1222 degrees Celsius. Each firing runs about thirty hours including cooling, and we fire when a kiln is full, not when a pot is ready.

Can children come to the studio?

Eight and up at the wheel, five and up at the hand-building table. Under-twelves need an adult in the room, though not necessarily at a wheel.

What if nothing I make survives the kiln?

Occasionally a piece cracks in the kiln or a glaze does something we did not expect. If nothing of yours makes it, you get a free session. This happens perhaps twice a year.

Can I cancel or move a booking?

Move it once, free, with 48 hours' notice. Inside 48 hours we charge half, because the clay is already wedged and portioned and a wheel sat empty.

Do you fire work made elsewhere?

Yes, for stoneware bodies only, at 90 rupees per litre of kiln space. We will not fire anything we did not see wedged, because a piece with a trapped air pocket can take out everything else on the shelf.

Do you sell gift vouchers?

Yes, for any session, valid twelve months, sent as a card by post or as a PDF. The couples session is the one people buy most and the one people redeem least, which we find quietly funny.

Finding us

Look for the blue shutter.

Third Cross runs behind the bakery. We are the fourth shutter down, painted blue, usually open with a wheel visible from the street. If the shutter is down, the kilns are on and we are not there.
